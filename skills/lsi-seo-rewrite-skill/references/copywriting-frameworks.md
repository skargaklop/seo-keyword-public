# Copywriting Frameworks for SEO Content

Proven frameworks for creating human-sounding, conversion-oriented content that ranks well and engages readers.

## Purpose

This reference provides structured approaches to writing that eliminate AI-like patterns while ensuring content is specific, valuable, and memorable. These frameworks are particularly effective for e-commerce content where technical information must be transformed into compelling user benefits.

**Core Philosophy:** Great copywriting transfers energy from writer to reader. Every word must earn its place, and every claim must be backed by specifics.

---

## SUCKS Framework

The SUCKS framework ensures content targets the right reader with a clear, memorable message.

### S - Specific

**Write for ONE person, not a crowd.**

- Define your single reader persona before writing
- Know their struggles, goals, and language patterns
- If you write for everyone, you write for no one

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Who is my ONE reader?
- [ ] What specific problem are they solving right now?
- [ ] What words do they actually use to describe their problem?

**Example:**
- ❌ "This software helps businesses manage inventory"
- ✅ "This tool helps warehouse managers stop losing track of pallets during shift changes"

### U - Unique & Useful

**Does this change how they think, feel, or act?**

- **Unique:** They haven't seen this perspective before
- **Useful:** They can actually apply this information
- AI content often recycles common advice; human content offers fresh angles

**Checklist:**
- [ ] What will they think differently after reading?
- [ ] What will they feel differently?
- [ ] What specific action can they take now?
- [ ] Would this information genuinely surprise or help them?

### C - Clear, Curious, Conversational

**Clear:** Main idea is unmissable
**Curious:** Questions that make readers think
**Conversational:** Reads like talking to a friend

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Could a 12-year-old understand the main point?
- [ ] Am I asking questions that provoke thought?
- [ ] Would I say this aloud to a friend?
- [ ] Is there unnecessary formality or jargon?

### K - Kept Simple & Structured

**Simple ideas, simple words. Clear structure.**

- Use common words instead of academic language
- Organize with clear beginning, middle, and end
- Each paragraph should advance one main idea

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Are there simpler words I could use?
- [ ] Does each paragraph have one clear purpose?
- [ ] Is the structure obvious (could I outline it easily)?

### S - Sticky

**Memorable phrases they'll repeat and remember.**

- Create quotable lines using sticky sentence techniques
- Ideas should lodge in memory beyond the reading session
- Test: Could they repeat your main point to a friend tomorrow?

**Checklist:**
- [ ] What one sentence should they remember?
- [ ] Did I use sticky techniques on key points?
- [ ] Would they want to share this?

## FAB Framework

Transform dry product specifications into compelling user benefits.

### Features (What is it?)

**State the fact about the product.**

This is where technical LSI terms integrate naturally. Features are objective facts about what the product is or has.

**Structure:** "[Product] [feature verb] [technical specification/LSI term]"

**Examples:**
- "The jacket uses three-layer Gore-Tex membrane with sealed seams"
- "This camera captures 24.2 megapixel images with ISO sensitivity up to 102,400"
- "The battery provides 12 hours of continuous playback via USB-C charging"

**LSI Integration:** Technical specifications, industry terminology, product category language

### Attributes (Why it matters?)

**What makes this feature important or unique?**

Connect the feature to meaningful differentiation with metrics when possible. This is where you explain why the feature matters in the context of user needs.

**Structure:** "This [feature] [provides capability/metric] that [distinguishes from typical alternatives]"

**Examples:**
- "This material withstands 20,000mm water column pressure and blocks storm-force winds"
- "The sensor size captures 2.3x more light than crop-frame cameras for cleaner low-light images"
- "Quick-charge delivers 50% battery in 15 minutes—faster than most competitors"

**LSI Integration:** Comparison terminology, performance metrics, differentiation language

### Benefits (What does it give the user?)

**The final user outcome or experience.**

Describe the concrete result the user experiences. This is where behavioral modifier LSI terms integrate naturally.

**Structure:** "You [experience outcome] even in [challenging scenario]"

**Examples:**
- "You stay completely warm and dry even during multi-day trekking in heavy rain"
- "Your night sports photos look sharp without grain or blur when venues limit flash"
- "Your playlist lasts through cross-country flights without hunting for outlets"

**LSI Integration:** User experience terms, behavioral modifiers, outcome language

## Complete FAB Application Examples

### Product: Waterproof Running Shoes

**Feature:** "The shoe uses Gore-Tex Invisible Fit technology with a seamless mesh upper"

**Attribute:** "This construction blocks water entry while maintaining breathability rated at 12,000 g/m²/24h—significantly higher than standard waterproof membranes"

**Benefit:** "Your feet stay dry through stream crossings and storm runs without overheating on summer pavement"

### Product: Professional Monitor

**Feature:** "This 27-inch display covers 99% of Adobe RGB color space with Delta E <2 accuracy"

**Attribute:** "The IPS panel with hardware calibration maintains color consistency across the entire surface, unlike cheaper TN alternatives with visible shift at viewing angles"

**Benefit:** "Your print output matches your screen exactly, eliminating wasted test prints and client revision rounds"

### Product: Kitchen Knife Set

**Feature:** "The blades are forged from German X50CrMoV15 steel with 58-60 HRC hardness"

**Attribute:** "This alloy composition balances edge retention with ease of sharpening—unlike softer steel that dulls quickly or harder steel that requires professional sharpening"

**Benefit:** "Your knives stay sharp through months of daily meal prep and restore to razor edges with just a few honing steel strokes"

## Sticky Sentence Techniques

Create memorable statements using these proven rhetorical devices.

### Alliteration

**Same starting sounds create rhythm and recall.**

- "Specificity is the secret"
- "Price promotes procrastination"
- "Better breakpoints boost business"
- "Focus fixes failures"
- "Simple sells, complex confuses"

**In SEO Content:** Use on 2-3 key points per page to make core concepts memorable.

### Symmetry

**Parallel structure creates satisfying balance.**

- "Read for awareness. Write for understanding."
- "It's not 10,000 hours. It's 10,000 iterations."
- "Features explain. Benefits sell."
- "Research informs. Testing confirms."
- "Products change. Problems remain."

**In SEO Content:** Effective for contrasting concepts or showing progression.

### Contrast

**Opposing ideas create impact through juxtaposition.**

- "To be everywhere is to be nowhere."
- "Be clear, not clever. Concise, not complex."
- "More features, more confusion. Less features, more focus."
- "The cheap becomes expensive. The expensive becomes cheap."
- "Speed without quality is waste. Quality without speed is irrelevant."

**In SEO Content:** Use when differentiating your product from competitors or approaches.

### Rhyme

**Similar ending sounds increase memorability.**

- "Tell a story or lose your glory"
- "Price that's nice, not merely twice"
- "Specs without checks are only wrecks"

**In SEO Content:** Use sparingly—appropriate for taglines or key differentiators, less so for technical content.

### Rhythm

**Pleasing cadence makes content flow and stick.**

- "When you can't wait to share it, they can't help but read it"
- "The right choice at the right time makes every time the right time"
- "What works wonders sometimes wanders"

**In SEO Content:** Test by reading aloud—rhythm issues become obvious when spoken.

### Combining Techniques

**For maximum stickiness, layer techniques:**

- "Anger prepares us to fight. Fear prepares us to flee." (Symmetry + Alliteration)
- "Features fade. Benefits stick." (Contrast + Rhyme + Parallelism)
- "Simple sells. Speed succeeds. Scales soar." (Alliteration + Symmetry)

## Quality Checklists

### Energy Transfer Test

**Does your writing transfer conviction or formality?**

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Does this sound like a real person's beliefs?
- [ ] Is there unnecessary formality or academic language?
- [ ] Could I imagine saying this to a friend?
- [ ] Does the tone convey confidence rather than hedging?
- [ ] Are corporate buzzwords replaced with plain language?

**Fix weak forms:**
- ❌ "Our approach incorporates multiple methodologies designed to accommodate diverse requirements"
- ✅ "We adapt to how you work"

### Undeniability Test

**Transform vague claims into concrete specifics.**

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Can I back this claim with specific numbers, examples, or names?
- [ ] Did I replace "many/some/significant" with actual quantities?
- [ ] Are there anecdotes or case studies supporting general claims?
- [ ] Did I avoid vague superlatives ("best," "amazing") without proof?

**Fix vague claims:**
- ❌ "How to boost your results by at least 87% in just one month"
- ✅ "This year, I helped 13 clients boost their results by 87%-256% in just one month. Here's how:"

### Read-Aloud Test

**Your ears catch problems your eyes miss.**

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Did I read the entire piece aloud?
- [ ] Do any sentences stumble or feel awkward when spoken?
- [ ] Is the sentence length varied or monotonous?
- [ ] Are there places where I run out of breath?
- [ ] Does it sound like a conversation or a lecture?

**Fix rhythm issues:**
- ❌ "The multifaceted nature of contemporary paradigms, which encompasses diverse modalities and approaches, necessitates a comprehensive reevaluation of traditional methodologies."
- ✅ "Things have changed. We need to rethink our approach."

### Specifics-over-Abstractions Test

**Specifics persuade. Abstractions bore.**

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Did I replace abstract nouns with concrete examples?
- [ ] Are there specific names, numbers, places, or quotes?
- [ ] Do examples illustrate general principles?
- [ ] Did I remove vague concepts that can't be visualized?

**Fix abstractions:**
- ❌ "Our customers improve significantly"
- ✅ "Users improve by 40% in 6 months, averaging 2.3 more closed deals per month"

## Integration with SEO Workflows

### When Writing Product Descriptions

1. **Apply SUCKS first:** Define the ONE buyer and what changes for them
2. **Use FAB structure:** Transform specs into benefits with LSI integration
3. **Add sticky sentences:** Make key differentiators memorable (2-3 per product)
4. **Run quality tests:** Energy, Undeniability, Read-Aloud, Specifics

### When Writing Category Content

1. **SUCKS:** Target the specific researcher/buyer at this stage
2. **FAB:** Explain category features, differentiation, and user outcomes
3. **Sticky:** Make category selection criteria memorable
4. **Tests:** Ensure guidance is concrete and actionable

### When Writing Blog Content

1. **SUCKS:** Define the specific learner/seeker
2. **FAB:** Present concepts as features with practical benefits
3. **Sticky:** Create memorable frameworks or principles
4. **Tests:** Verify examples are specific and actionable

## Common Anti-Patterns to Avoid

### Empty Specificity

Using specific-seeming language without actual specifics:
- ❌ "Our comprehensive solution delivers transformative results"
- ✅ "Our 3-step process reduces inventory errors by 73% in 90 days"

### Fake Benefits

Features disguised as benefits without user outcome:
- ❌ "Benefit: Includes 10GB storage" (This is a feature)
- ✅ "Benefit: Store 5,000+ photos without managing cloud subscriptions"

### Forced Stickiness

Sticky techniques that call attention to themselves:
- ❌ "Super storage saves serious sums" (too much)
- ✅ "Storage you don't have to manage" (natural rhythm)
