# Blog Post Template

SEO-optimized blog post structure with LSI integration, human-sounding prose, and anti-AI pattern avoidance.

## H1 Formula

**Primary Keyword + Hook/Angle + Article Type Signal**

**Length:** 50-70 characters maximum for optimal display in SERPs

**Examples:**
- "How to Choose Waterproof Running Shoes (Complete Guide)"
- "SEO Best Practices for E-commerce Sites in 2024"
- "When to Replace Running Shoes: Signs You Need New Gear"
- "Product Photography Setup Guide for Small Business"
- "LSI Keywords: What They Are and How to Use Them"

**Formula components:**
1. **Primary keyword** - Main search term you're targeting
2. **Hook/angle** - What makes this article unique (complete guide, beginner tips, expert advice)
3. **Type signal** - Tells readers what to expect (guide, tutorial, checklist, comparison)

**Forbidden patterns:**
- No clickbait or sensationalist claims
- No keyword stuffing at the end
- No bracketed calls-to-action like [Updated 2024]

## Article Structure

### Hook/Lead (100-150 words)

**Purpose:** Grab attention, state the article's value, and include the primary keyword naturally.

**Structure:**
```markdown
[Specific claim or attention-grabbing statement with data or example].

[Context about why this matters to the reader].

[Article promise - what they will learn or be able to do after reading].

[Primary keyword appears naturally in first 100 words].
```

**Requirements:**
- Start with a specific claim, statistic, or compelling statement
- Include primary keyword within first 100 words
- State what the reader will gain from reading
- Use 1-2 LSI terms naturally
- NO forbidden openers ("In today's fast-paced world," "In an era where")
- Write directly to ONE reader (SUCKS framework)

**Example:**
```markdown
Running shoes lose shock absorption after 300-500 miles. Most runners don't notice the gradual decline until knee pain sets in.

Knowing when to replace your shoes prevents injury and keeps training consistent. This guide shows you the exact signs that your shoes are worn out, how to check them, and what to look for in your next pair.
```

---

### H2: [Key Question] (PAA-derived)

**Purpose:** Answer the primary question searchers are asking, based on People Also Ask (PAA) data.

**Structure:**
```markdown
**H2: [Primary PAA Question]**

[Direct answer in first sentence].

[Supporting detail with 2-3 LSI terms integrated naturally].

[Example, data point, or specific scenario].

[Actionable guidance or next step].
```

**LSI Integration:**
- Include 2-3 LSI terms per section
- Place LSI terms in: first paragraph, subheadings, examples, and before concluding sentence
- Never force keywords - they must read naturally

**Requirements:**
- Answer the question directly (no "it depends" hedging)
- Provide specific examples or data when possible
- Include actionable guidance
- 100-200 words per H2 section

**Example:**
```markdown
**H2: When Should You Replace Running Shoes?**

Replace running shoes every 300-500 miles or when shock absorption degrades visibly. Most runners need new shoes every 4-6 months with regular training.

Your shoes wear out faster if you run on concrete, weigh more, or have a heavy stride. Track your mileage from purchase date and inspect the midsole compression regularly.

Visible signs include compressed foam, uneven wear patterns, and creasing in the heel counter. If your knees start hurting during runs, check your shoes first.
```

---

### H2: [Secondary Key Question]

**Repeat the same structure** for each major question searchers need answered.

**Aim for 3-5 H2 sections** covering:
1. The primary question (most important)
2. Secondary questions from PAA
3. How-to guidance or process steps
4. Common mistakes or considerations
5. Comparison or decision factors

---

### H2: Frequently Asked Questions

**Purpose:** Capture long-tail traffic and address specific concerns.

**Structure:**
```markdown
**H2: Frequently Asked Questions**

**Q: [PAA Question 1]?**
A: [Direct answer with 1-2 LSI terms]. [Supporting detail in 1-2 sentences].

**Q: [PAA Question 2]?**
A: [Direct answer with 1-2 LSI terms]. [Supporting detail in 1-2 sentences].

[Continue with 3-6 FAQ questions]
```

**FAQ Requirements:**
- Use actual questions from SERP PAA section
- Answer directly (50-80 words per answer)
- Include LSI terms where relevant
- Cover objections, edge cases, and clarifications
- Make answers skimmable and actionable

---

### Conclusion (50-100 words)

**Purpose:** Summarize key points and provide a clear next step.

**Structure:**
```markdown
[Key point summary in 1-2 sentences].

[Optional: CTA if relevant to article purpose].

[NO forbidden closers: "In conclusion," "To summarize," "In summary"].
```

**Requirements:**
- Summarize the main takeaway
- Provide next step or CTA only if genuinely useful
- 50-100 words maximum
- End with strong, specific statement
- NO "in conclusion" or similar forbidden closers

**Forbidden patterns:**
- "In conclusion," "To summarize," "In summary"
- "Ultimately," "At the end of the day"
- "Now that you know..." followed by generic advice
- Restating the entire article

**Example:**
```markdown
Check your shoes every 300 miles. Inspect the midsole for compression, test shock absorption by pressing on the heel, and replace when you see visible creasing.

Your knees will tell you when your shoes are done - listen to them.
```

## Blog-Specific LSI Integration

### LSI Categories for Blog Posts

**1. Educational/Concept Terminology**
- Definitions and explanations
- Industry-specific terms
- Technical concepts related to the topic
- Beginner vs. advanced concepts

**2. Industry Trend Language**
- Current developments in the field
- Emerging practices or technologies
- Standards and best practices
- Comparison to outdated methods

**3. Problem/Solution Terms**
- Pain point language
- Symptom descriptions
- Solution categories
- Outcome and benefit language

**4. Expertise Signals**
- Credentials and qualifications
- Experience indicators
- Authoritativeness markers
- Trust signals

### LSI Distribution for Blog Posts

**Target density:** 8-12 LSI terms per 1,000 words

**Placement priority:**
1. H1 and first 100 words (primary keyword mandatory)
2. H2/H3 headings (secondary keywords)
3. First paragraph of each section
4. Examples and specific scenarios
5. FAQ answers
6. Before concluding statements

**Integration rules:**
- Never force keywords into awkward phrasing
- Use natural variations and synonyms
- Include long-tail conversational phrases
- Balance keyword density with readability

## Sticky Phrase Placement

**Goal:** Create 2-3 memorable statements that readers will remember and share.

### Sticky Sentence Techniques

**Alliteration** - Same starting sounds
- "Specificity is the secret"
- "Perfect practice prevents poor performance"

**Symmetry** - Parallel structure
- "Read for awareness. Write for understanding."
- "Plan your work. Work your plan."

**Contrast** - Opposing ideas
- "To be everywhere is to be nowhere."
- "Focus on one thing, not everything."

**Rhythm** - Pleasing cadence
- "When you can't wait to share it, they can't help but read it"

**Where to place sticky phrases:**
1. Opening hook (1 sticky phrase max)
2. Key insight within main H2 sections
3. Closing statement (for lasting impression)

**Example sticky phrases for blog posts:**
- "Check your shoes every 300 miles" (specific, actionable, memorable)
- "Listen to your knees, not your calendar" (contrast + advice)
- "Good shoes cost less than bad injuries" (contrast + truth)

## Meta Tags Generation

### Title Tag (50-60 characters)

**Formula:** Primary Keyword + Hook + Modifiers

**Examples:**
- "How to Choose Running Shoes | Complete Guide 2024"
- "When to Replace Running Shoes: Signs and Mileage Guide"
- "Best Running Shoes for Flat Feet | Podiatrist-Recommended"

**Requirements:**
- 50-60 characters maximum
- Primary keyword at the beginning
- Include year for time-sensitive content
- No keyword stuffing or repetition
- Include modifiers that add value (guide, checklist, tutorial)

### Meta Description (150-160 characters)

**Formula:** Problem statement + Solution + CTA

**Example:**
```text
Running shoes lose cushioning after 300-500 miles. Learn the signs of wear, how to check shock absorption, and when to replace your shoes to prevent injury.
```

**Requirements:**
- 150-160 characters maximum
- Include primary keyword naturally
- State the benefit clearly
- Include a soft CTA (learn, discover, find)
- No clickbait or sensationalist claims
- Make it compelling but accurate

### URL Structure

**Formula:** /blog/[primary-keyword]/[optional-modifier]

**Examples:**
- /blog/when-to-replace-running-shoes/
- /blog/how-to-choose-running-shoes-guide/
- /blog/running-shoe-mileage-limits/

**Requirements:**
- Short, descriptive URLs
- Include primary keyword
- Use hyphens between words
- Lowercase only
- No stop words (a, the, and) unless necessary for clarity

### LSI Integration

- [ ] Secondary keywords in H2/H3 headings
- [ ] LSI terms placed in priority positions
- [ ] No forced or awkward keyword placement

### Anti-AI Pattern Avoidance
- [ ] NO correlative constructions ("X aren't just Y, they're Z")
- [ ] NO "just" overuse (max once per 500 words)
- [ ] NO "actually" overuse (max once per 500 words)
- [ ] NO hedge words (might, could, perhaps, seems like)
- [ ] NO forbidden openers ("In today's fast-paced world")
- [ ] NO forbidden closers ("In conclusion," "To summarize")
- [ ] NO rhetorical flourishes ("The best part?...")
- [ ] NO staccato phrasing ("Simple. Clear. Effective.")
- [ ] NO em dashes (use regular hyphens with spaces)

### SUCKS Framework Application
- [ ] **S**pecific: Written for ONE reader type
- [ ] **U**nique: Contains a unique angle or insight
- [ ] **C**lear: Main idea is unmissable
- [ ] **K**ept Simple: 12-year-old can understand main points
- [ ] **S**ticky: 2-3 memorable phrases included

- [ ] Answers the primary question directly
- [ ] Provides specific examples or data
- [ ] Includes actionable guidance
- [ ] Has clear structure and flow
- [ ] Energy transfers (not formal or academic)
- [ ] Reads like conversation, not lecture

- [ ] Title tag is 50-60 characters
- [ ] Meta description is 150-160 characters
- [ ] URL includes primary keyword
- [ ] Meta description includes CTA
- [ ] Title tag starts with primary keyword

### Human-Sounding Prose
- [ ] Sounds like a real person talking
- [ ] Would pass read-aloud test
- [ ] No corporate jargon or buzzwords
- [ ] Specific over abstract language
- [ ] Confident statements (no hedging)
- [ ] Varied sentence structure and rhythm

## Before/After Example

### Before (AI-Generated):
```markdown
# The Importance of Choosing the Right Running Shoes

In today's fast-paced world of fitness and athletics, selecting the appropriate footwear has become more crucial than ever. Running shoes aren't just about comfort - they're about performance, injury prevention, and longevity in your sport.

When it comes to choosing the perfect pair of running shoes, there are several factors to consider. The right shoes can make a significant difference in your running experience.

## Key Features to Look For

Cushioning technology has evolved significantly over the years. Modern running shoes incorporate advanced materials that provide superior shock absorption.

To summarize, choosing the right running shoes is essential for any serious runner.
```

### After (Human-Sounding + SEO-Optimized):
```markdown
# How to Choose Running Shoes for Injury Prevention (Complete Guide)

Running shoes lose shock absorption after 300-500 miles. Most runners don't notice the gradual decline until knee pain sets in - then they wonder why their workouts feel harder.

This guide shows you how to pick shoes that match your stride, prevent injury, and last through their full mileage range.

## What Your Shoes Actually Do

Your shoes handle three jobs: absorb shock, stabilize your foot, and grip surfaces. Most shoe marketing focuses on cushioning, but stability matters more for injury prevention.

Check the wear pattern on your old shoes. Even wear means good alignment. Inner-edge wear means you overpronate and need stability features.

## When to Replace Them

Replace shoes every 300-500 miles or when the midsole compresses visibly. Press your thumb into the heel - if it doesn't bounce back, the foam is dead.

Your knees will tell you when your shoes are done. Shin splints and knee pain often mean worn-out cushioning, not bad form.

## Budget vs. Features

Expensive shoes don't prevent injuries better - the right shoe for your foot does. Motion control shoes hurt neutral runners, and neutral shoes wreck overpronators.

Get fitted at a specialty running store. Watch your stride on a treadmill, then test 3-5 pairs within your budget range.

Good shoes cost less than bad injuries.
```

**What changed:**
- Removed "In today's fast-paced world" (forbidden opener)
- Removed correlative construction ("aren't just X - they're Y")
- Added specific data (300-500 miles)
- Replaced vague "significant difference" with concrete guidance
- Eliminated "To summarize" forbidden closer
- Added sticky phrase: "Good shoes cost less than bad injuries"
- Applied SUCKS framework (specific, clear, kept simple)
- Integrated LSI terms naturally (shock absorption, overpronate, stability features)
